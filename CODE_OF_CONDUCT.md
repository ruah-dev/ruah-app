# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Ruah (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and
any other project space) to be a harassment-free experience for everyone,
whatever their age, body, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic
status, nationality, appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual
identity and orientation. We act in ways that keep this community open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive and healthy.

## Our standards

Behaviour that helps:

- being kind and patient, especially with people who are new;
- respecting different opinions, experiences and ways of working;
- giving and gracefully taking constructive feedback about the work;
- owning our mistakes, apologising to those affected and learning from them;
- focusing on what is best for the people who use and build Ruah.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind;
- trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks;
- public or private harassment;
- publishing someone else's private information (an address, an email, a
  screenshot of their account) without their explicit permission;
- anything else a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in every project space, and when someone represents the
project in public (an official account, a talk, an event).

## Reporting and enforcement

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it privately to
the maintainers: open the repository's **Security** tab → **Report a
vulnerability** and start the title with "Code of conduct:" (the report is
visible only to the maintainers), or use GitHub's **Report content** on the
comment itself. Do not open a public issue about it.

Maintainers will review every report promptly and fairly, keep the reporter's
identity private, and take the action they judge appropriate: from a private
note asking someone to change their behaviour, through a public warning or a
temporary ban from project spaces, up to a permanent ban for sustained or
severe harassment. Maintainers who do not follow this code in good faith may
face the same consequences.
